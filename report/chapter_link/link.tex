\section{Ports and parameters}

\section{Equations of Motion}

The Newton--Euler equations for link $i$, written in the body frame attached to the link, are given by.
Denoting the generalised momentum as
\begin{equation}
  p_{\text{lin}} = m\,v_G, \qquad h_{\text{ang}} = J\,\omega,
\end{equation}
one obtains the state-space form that 20-sim integrates directly:
\begin{align}
  \dot{p}_{\text{lin}} &= f^{\text{ext}} - m\,\omega \times v_G, \\
  \dot{h}_{\text{ang}} &= \tau^{\text{ext}} - \omega \times J\omega, \\
  v_G &= p_{\text{lin}} / m, \\
  \omega &= J^{-1} h_{\text{ang}}.
\end{align}

\subsection{Body Submodel}
The Body submodel is an I-element that integrates the Newton--Euler equations. It is an effort-in flow-out element, meaning that it receives the total wrench acting on the body and outputs the linear and angular velocities at the centre of mass.
For these reasons it is connected to a one junction that sums all efforts, after transforming them to the CoM via the MTF elements, and outputs the body velocity that is fed back to the MTFs for the velocity shift.
The submodel implements the equations of motion as described above.

\section{Bond-Graph Structure}

The \texttt{Link} sub-model exposes two \emph{power ports} of dimension six:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{wrench\_i}: the power applied seen from reference frame of the i-th link. It is seen as the one exiting from link $i$ and entering link $i$.
  \item \texttt{wrench\_ipp}: it is also seen as the one exiting from link $i$ and entering link $i+1$. It is the wrench applied by the link on the next link.
\end{itemize}
It also receives a signal input \texttt{T\_next}, the homogeneous transformation, used to orient gravity.

Internally the model contains:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Two \textbf{MTF} elements (\texttt{i\_com} and \texttt{ipp\_com}) that shift the reference from the link origins to the centre of mass.
  \item A \textbf{1-junction} that enforces the common-velocity constraint at the centre of mass and sums all efforts.
  \item The \textbf{Body} I-element that integrates the Newton--Euler equations described above.
  \item A \textbf{MSe} element that injects the gravitational wrench.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Gravity Source (MSe)}

The gravitational force in the inertial frame is $f_g = [0,\,0,\,-mg]^T$.
To express it in the body frame of link $i$, the rotation matrix $\prescript{0}{}{i}R \in SO(3)$ extracted from \texttt{T\_next} (namely, $\prescript{0}{}{T}_i$) is used:
\begin{equation}
  \prescript{body}{}{f}_g = \prescript{0}{}{R}_i^\top \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\-mg\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \prescript{body}{}{\tau}_g = \mathbf{0}.
\end{equation}
This wrench is injected at the 1-junction so that it acts on the body mass.

\subsection{MTF Equations}
In the developed architecture, the physical rigid-body transformation across the link's length is entirely handled by the adjacent \texttt{Joint} sub-models. 
Consequently, both the input and output power ports of the \texttt{Link} sub-model are referenced to the same spatial location: the local origin of frame $i$.
Let $W = [f, \tau]^T$ be the generic wrench and $\nu = [v, \omega]^T$ be the generic twist. The mathematical relations governing the power-preserving transformation from the port origin to the CoM are:
\begin{align}
  v_{\text{port}} &= v_{\text{G}} + r_{GP} \times \omega_{\text{G}}, \\
  \tau_{\text{G}} &= \tau_{\text{G}} - r_{GP} \times f_{\text{G}}.
\end{align}

Based on the bond-graph causalities, the two MTF blocks implement these equations as follows.

\subsection{Causality Assignment}

Both power ports carry \emph{fixed integral causality}.
This means:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Efforts (forces/torques at the links) are \emph{given} by the connected joints.
  \item Velocities (flows) are \emph{computed} by the body integrator.
\end{itemize}
The 1-junction has one element with \emph{effort-out} causality (the Body I-element) and all others with \emph{effort-in} causality (the two MTF connections and the MSe source).
