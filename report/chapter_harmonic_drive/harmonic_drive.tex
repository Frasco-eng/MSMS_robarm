\section{Introduction}

Robotic joints are not directly driven: a gearbox with a high reduction ratio sits between the motor shaft and the output link.
In the UR5, \emph{harmonic-drive} (strain-wave) gearboxes are used.
These components exhibit several non-ideal characteristics that introduce dynamic effects that affect the closed-loop performance:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item High gear ratio with near-zero backlash.
  \item Non-linear torsional stiffness (the flex-spline deforms elastically).
  \item Viscous friction at the input shaft.
  \item Asymmetric Coulomb friction (static friction differs for positive and negative rotation).
  \item Motor rotor inertia reflected through the gear ratio.
  \item Internal structural damping within the flex-spline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Bond-Graph Structure}

The \texttt{HarmonicDrive} sub-model has:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{motor}: scalar power port input.
  \item \texttt{harmonic\_drive}: scalar power port output.
\end{itemize}

The topology is shown schematically in the bond graph below.
The key elements are:

\paragraph{Gearbox inertia (I element).}
The harmonic drive inertia $J_{\text{rotor}}$ stores kinetic energy:
\begin{equation}
  J\,\dot{\omega}_m = \tau_m - \tau_{\text{friction}} - \tau_{\text{elastic}}/N,
\end{equation}
where $\omega_m$ is the motor angular velocity and $N$ is the gear ratio (negative due to sign convention, default $N=-0.01$, i.e.\ ratio of 100).

\paragraph{Gear ratio (TF element).}
The transformer element with ratio $N$ enforces:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_m = N \, \tau_{\text{out}}, \qquad \omega_{\text{out}} = N \, \omega_m.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Torsional stiffness (C element -- smooth cubic).}
The flex-spline introduces a non-linear torsional compliance.
The constitutive law is implemented as a smooth cubic polynomial that interpolates the three stiffness breakpoints:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_s(\varphi) = a\,\varphi^3 + b\,\varphi,
\end{equation}
where $\varphi = \int(\omega_{\text{out}} - \omega_{\text{load}})\,dt$ is the elastic twist of the flex-spline, and the coefficients $a$ and $b$ are computed from the three-zone stiffness data via:
\begin{align}
  \varphi_1 &= \frac{T_1}{k_1}, \qquad \varphi_2 = \varphi_1 + \frac{T_2 - T_1}{k_2}, \\
  \Delta &= \varphi_1\,\varphi_2\bigl(\varphi_1^2 - \varphi_2^2\bigr), \\
  a &= \frac{\varphi_2\,T_1 - \varphi_1\,T_2}{\Delta}, \qquad
  b = \frac{-\varphi_2^3\,T_1 + \varphi_1^3\,T_2}{\Delta},
\end{align}


\paragraph{Viscous and Coulomb friction at the motor shaft (R element).}
At the motor-side 1-junction (input shaft), a combined resistance element captures both viscous damping and asymmetric Coulomb friction:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_f(\omega) = \frac{b_{f,+} - b_{f,-}}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\!\Bigl(\omega - \tan\!\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{b_{f,+}+b_{f,-}}{b_{f,+}-b_{f,-}}\Bigr)\Bigr)
  + \frac{b_{f,+}+b_{f,-}}{2}
  + b_w\,\omega,
\end{equation}
with $b_{f,+} = 0.008$~Nm and $b_{f,-} = -0.007$~Nm, and $b_w = 6.4\times10^{-4}$~Nm\,s/rad.
The $\arctan$ regularisation avoids a discontinuity at zero velocity and ensures numerical stability.

\paragraph{Asymmetric Coulomb friction at the output shaft (R element).}
An identical resistance formulation is applied at the output-side 1-junction with the same parameters $(b_{f,+},\, b_{f,-},\, b_w)$, capturing friction at the output bearing of the strain-wave gear.

\paragraph{Structural damping of the flex-spline (R element).}
A third resistance element is placed at the compliance 1-junction, in parallel with the torsional spring C element.
This models the internal structural damping of the flex-spline itself: as the flex-spline elastically deforms, part of the stored energy is dissipated through material hysteresis.
The resistance is governed by the same smooth regularised formula:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{d}(\dot{\varphi}) = B_s \cdot \frac{2}{\pi}\arctan(\dot{\varphi}) + b_w\,\dot{\varphi},
\end{equation}
where $\dot{\varphi} = \omega_{\text{out}} - \omega_{\text{load}}$ is the relative angular velocity across the flex-spline,
$B_s = 0.4$~Nm is the symmetric Coulomb-like structural damping coefficient (i.e.\ $B_{s,+} = 0.4$~Nm, $B_{s,-} = -0.4$~Nm), and $b_w$ is the same viscous coefficient shared with the shaft friction elements.
Because this element is on the compliance branch, its effort opposes the rate of elastic deformation, adding dissipation proportional to how rapidly the flex-spline is being twisted.
This element is essential to damp the high-frequency oscillations associated with the elastic flex-spline mode that would otherwise persist indefinitely under a purely conservative C element.

\subsection{Causality Assignment}

The \texttt{motor} port is fixed \emph{effort-in}: the motor torque source imposes effort.
The \texttt{harmonic\_drive} port is fixed \emph{effort-out}: the transmission output imposes torque on the joint.
Within the sub-model:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The 1-junction at the motor shaft has the I-element as the \emph{effort-out} element (integral causality), and the TF and R elements as effort-in elements.
  \item The 0-junction at the output shaft is the sum point for the elastic torque from the compliance branch, the structural damping torque, and the output Coulomb friction.
  \item The C element is in integral causality: $\dot{\varphi} = f_C$, $e_C = \tau_s(\varphi)$.
  \item The structural damping R on the compliance branch shares the same 1-junction as the C element; it therefore sees the same flow $\dot{\varphi}$ and contributes an additional dissipative effort in parallel with the spring torque.
\end{itemize}
