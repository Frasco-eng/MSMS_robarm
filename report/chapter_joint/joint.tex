\section{Physical Description}

Each of the six joints of the UR5 is a revolute joint.
In the bond-graph model, the \texttt{Joint} sub-model is responsible for:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Performing the DH frame transformation between the inbound and outbound twist ports.
  \item Extracting the scalar joint position and velocity $(q_i, \dot{q}_i) \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, 6$
  \item Receiving the scalar actuator torque $\tau_i \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, 6$ and transmitting it to the mechanical chain.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The MTF Element as a DH Transformer}

The core of the \texttt{Joint} model is an MTF element that implements the DH transformation.
Given the joint angle $\theta_i = q_i + \tilde{q}_i$ and the stored parameters, the rotation matrix $R_i$ and translation $P_i$ are computed as follows:
\begin{align}
  R_i &= \begin{pmatrix}
    \cos\theta_i & -\sin\theta_i\cos\alpha_i &  \sin\theta_i\sin\alpha_i \\
    \sin\theta_i &  \cos\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & -\cos\theta_i\sin\alpha_i \\
    0            &  \sin\alpha_i             &  \cos\alpha_i
  \end{pmatrix}, \\[6pt]
  P_i &= \begin{pmatrix}
    a_i\cos\theta_i \\ a_i\sin\theta_i \\ d_i
  \end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
The MTF enforces power continuity by transforming twists and wrenches:
\begin{align}
  \tau_{\text{ipp}} &= R_i^T \tau_i, \\
  f_{\text{ipp}}    &= R_i^T f_i - R_i^T (P_i \times \tau_i), \\
  \omega_i          &= R_i\,\omega_{\text{ipp}}, \\
  v_i               &= P_i \times R_i\,\omega_{\text{ipp}} + R_i\,v_{\text{ipp}}.
\end{align}
Simultaneously, the MTF outputs the homogeneous transformation matrix $T_i$ as a signal to be used by the 3D animation tool for the visual representation of the kinematic chain.

\section{Joint Kinematics and Constraint Elements}

A revolute pair restricts five of the six possible degrees of freedom between two adjacent links, allowing only a relative rotation about a single axis. In the adopted bond-graph topology, this physical restriction is modeled by splitting the 6-dimensional twist variables using \texttt{PowerDemux} elements and selectively applying velocity constraints. This allows to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Split the 3D translational velocity components} into $v_{i_x}$, $v_{i_y}$, and $v_{i_z}$.
  \item \textbf{Split the 3D angular velocity components} into $\omega_{i_x}$, $\omega_{i_y}$, and $\omega_{i_z}$.
\end{itemize}

Assuming the $z$-axis as the rotation axis, the constraints are enforced as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Translational Constraints}: The relative linear velocities along the $x$, $y$, and $z$ axes must be zero. This is achieved via a \texttt{0-junction} connected to a \texttt{Vel\_constr} element. The constraint acts as an infinite impedance, effectively outputting the exact reaction forces required to maintain zero translational flow between the links.
  \item \textbf{Rotational Constraints ($x$ and $y$ axes)}: Similarly, rotations about the $x$ and $y$ axes are blocked. Another \texttt{0-junction} connected to the \texttt{Omega\_constr} element imposes a zero-flow condition, generating the necessary reaction bending moments.
  \item \textbf{Actuated Rotational Degree of Freedom ($z$ axis)}: The $z$-component is the only unconstrained axis. A dedicated \texttt{0-junction} acts as the summation point for the efforts (torques) acting on this DoF. The \texttt{actuator} port, which receives the control torque computed by the controller, connects directly to this junction.
\end{itemize}

Finally, the joint state variables are extracted from the power flow of this unconstrained axis: a \textbf{FlowSensor} reads the scalar rotational velocity $\dot{q}_i$ (which corresponds to $\omega_z$), and an \textbf{Integrator} computes the joint position $q_i = \int \dot{q}_i\,dt$ to be fed into the MTF for the DH transformation.


\section{Causality Assignment and Complexity}

The joint sub-model presents the most complex causality situation in the entire robot model:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The inbound port \texttt{twist\_i} has \emph{fixed effort-out} causality: the joint \emph{imposes} force/torque on the previous link and \emph{receives} velocity from it.
  \item The outbound port \texttt{twist\_ipp} has \emph{fixed effort-out} causality as well: the joint imposes force/torque on the next link.
  \item The MTF has a \emph{causality constraint}: the two sides must have opposite causality (one effort-in, one effort-out).
  \item The constraint elements (\texttt{Vel\_constr} for translational DoFs, \texttt{Omega\_constr} for $xy$-rotation) set effort from flow via an algebraic constraint equation, implying \emph{derivative causality} on the constrained directions.
  \item The \texttt{actuator} port has \emph{fixed-in causality}: the motor effort is imposed externally, so the 0-junction for $q_z$ has \emph{one effort-in} element (the actuator).
\end{itemize}

The combination of fixed causality on both external power ports and the internal MTF constraint means that \emph{no free integral causality} can be assigned to the internal inertia elements of the joint body; instead, these joints rely on the inertia of the connected links.
This is the correct physical interpretation: the joint itself has zero inertia in this model.
