\section{Test Scenario}

The simulation is designed to test all layers of the model simultaneously, covering both the dynamic response of the mechanical chain and the complete trajectory-tracking control stack:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Cartesian trajectory tracking}: the end-effector is commanded to follow a smooth six-dimensional Cartesian reference trajectory (position and orientation) generated offline via a PCHIP spline interpolant through a set of ten waypoints spanning 10 seconds of simulation.
  \item \textbf{Inverse kinematics}: at every time step, the desired end-effector pose is mapped to joint-space references by the closed-form UR5 IK solver. An unwrapping stage ensures continuity of the joint references across $2\pi$ boundaries.
  \item \textbf{Gravity compensation}: the arm must hold its posture against gravity at all times, including while carrying a load.
  \item \textbf{Harmonic drive effects}: the elastic flex-spline introduces a slight lag between the commanded and actual joint position.
  \item \textbf{Switchable payload}: a rigid load of approximately 1~kg is attached to and detached from the end-effector at prescribed times, controlled by the \texttt{Cond} flag embedded in the trajectory CSV.
\end{enumerate}

The joint initial conditions are set to match the IK solution at the first waypoint.

\section{Joint Position Tracking}

\Cref{fig:joint_pos} shows the time evolution of all six joint angles $q_i(t)$ compared to their references $q_i^*(t)$.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=1.0\textwidth]{chapter_results_conclusions/figs/joints_tracking.png}
  \caption{Joint position tracking}
  \label{fig:joint_pos}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Observation.}
All six joints track their time-varying references with a small steady-state error, consistent with the PID control law.
The tracking error remains bounded throughout the 10-second simulation, even across the load pick-up and release events.
Due to the uncertainty in the load mass, and its compensation through the integral term only, a small error is noticeable during the pickup phase, 
which is then gradually reduced as the integral action accumulates, to later be fully compensated at steady state.
No overshoots are present thanks to the derivative damping term and the gravity feedforward.

The delay between the reference and actual joint angles is around 0.1 seconds.

\begin{figure}[H]

  \caption{Joint control torques $\tau_i(t)$}
  \label{fig:joint_torque}

\paragraph{Observation.}
Torques are smooth and bounded with no sign of instability. As the trajectory time is 10s, the dynamics is not negligible and therefore the PID is very active compared to a pure position control scenario, where the torques would be mostly constant and equal to the gravity compensation term.

The close match between the steady-state commanded torque and the analytically predicted gravity torque confirms that the compensation term is correctly implemented.

\section{End-Effector Position: Kinematics vs.\ Dynamics}
%-------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=1.0\textwidth]{chapter_results_conclusions/figs/dynamics_proof.png}
  \caption{ comparison of end-effector XYZ position computed (i) directly from the bond-graph state via the DH forward kinematics chain (Transformation blocks) and (ii) from the body velocity integration in the Link sub-models}
  \label{fig:kinematics_vs_dynamics}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Observation.}
The end-effector position computed by forward kinematics through the Denavit--Hartenberg transformation chain and the position obtained by integrating the bond-graph body velocities within the Link sub-models are in very close agreement throughout the simulation, with a discrepancy of the order of numerical precision (below $10^{-8}$~m).
This internal consistency check confirms that the MTF-based wrench/twist propagation and the DH transformation chain are mutually coherent: the kinematic and dynamic layers of the model are correctly coupled.

\section{Load--End-Effector Constraint Verification}
%-------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=1.0\textwidth]{chapter_results_conclusions/figs/constraints.png}
  \caption{time evolution of the six-component constraint wrench at the X0 junction, and of the velocity difference between the end-effector port and the payload body during the load-attached phase}
  \label{fig:constraints}
\end{figure}


\paragraph{Observation.}
During the load-attached phase (controlled by the \texttt{Cond} flag), the velocity difference between the end-effector port and the payload body remains at the level of numerical noise ($<10^{-3}$~m/s), confirming that the very stiff C element ($c = 10^{-6}$) effectively enforces a rigid connection.
The constraint wrench correctly reflects the inertial and gravitational load of the $1\,\text{kg}$ payload: during acceleration phases the force component along the direction of motion increases accordingly, while at near-constant velocity the wrench is dominated by the gravity contribution.
At the load pick-up and release instants a brief transient is visible in the constraint wrench as the stiff spring absorbs the initial velocity mismatch; the additional R damper at the constraint junction suppresses this transient within a few simulation steps.

\section{Conclusions}
%=============================================================

A complete bond-graph model of the UR5 six-degree-of-freedom robot manipulator has been presented and validated.
Starting from the fundamental Newton--Euler equations of a rigid body expressed in terms of twists and wrenches, a hierarchical set of reusable 20-sim sub-models --- \texttt{Link}, \texttt{Joint}, \texttt{Transformation}, \texttt{HarmonicDrive}, \texttt{PID}, and \texttt{GravityTerm} --- was developed and assembled into a complete closed-loop trajectory tracking system.

The following aspects deserve particular emphasis:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \textbf{Modulated transformer} (MTF) is the central bond-graph element for both the change-of-reference-point in the Link model and the DH transformation in the Joint model.
  \item \textbf{Causality assignment} is the most challenging design step and it's possible to maintain a full integral causality of all the elements thanks to constraint blocks, used to interface two rigid bodies. 
  \item The \textbf{GravityTerm} block, while signal-only, is critical for controller stability: without feedforward compensation, the PID controller cannot hold arbitrary postures.
  \item The \textbf{structural damping} added in parallel with the flex-spline compliance is necessary to obtain physically plausible and numerically well-behaved harmonic drive dynamics; without it, the elastic mode persists indefinitely.
  \item The \textbf{switchable load} mechanism based on the \texttt{SwitchingZeroJunction} and the trajectory \texttt{Cond} flag elegantly handles the pick-and-place scenario while preserving integral causality throughout, requiring no modification of the underlying bond-graph topology.
  \item The \textbf{closed-form IK solver} combined with a phase-unwrapping stage provides smooth, continuous joint references directly from the Cartesian trajectory, closing the loop between the high-level task-space planner and the joint-level PID+G controller.
\end{itemize}
